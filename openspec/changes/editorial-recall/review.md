# Review record

Five independent agent specification reviews ran on 2026-10-10 against spec 49495e8 (issue #222). They are agent reviews, not approvals by named team members; owner approval and CI remain separate requirements. All five returned needs-changes; the resolutions below are in the revised spec, design, IDD and tasks.

| Reviewer | Main findings | Resolution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| QA / evaluation methodology | The dev slip and a held-out clean base came from the same article. Pooling 27 easy injected trials let the motivating misses persist while the bar passed. The rubrics are contaminated today (incident phrases are rubric examples). The held-out set was hidden from runs but not from the rubric author. Quote and category could be satisfied by different blockers, and the NASA fixture accepted `voice`. MUST NOT clauses had no clean tests. Order and separation could not be proven (squash merges, untraceable runs, undefined rounds, mixed baseline). The sample was small and confounded. Panel rules were weak. "Host-printed lines" was ambiguous. | The rubric author's fixtures become the dev set. A sealed held-out set by another author, with near-misses, has only its hash published before the change. There are separate bars for the #219 set, the motivating misses and each held-out class. Contamination is recorded and the examples removed. The pass rule requires span and category on one blocker, and the NASA fixture returns to `grounding`. The harness records hashes, revision, uncommitted changes and per-fixture results; `--final` is required and run reports are committed. A round is one rubric commit plus one dev run. The baseline is the release worktree run back to back. Panel votes are unanimous and committed. Only labels and the CTA are exempt in the guard. |
| Russian linguistics | "Context resolves it" exempts every motivating miss. The verifier had no working ground for exemptions (`understood-joke` never clears a language claim, and "correct as written" can be read as "grammatical"). «одеть куртку» is a rubric example and colloquially common. Same-field terms and role-changing government had no scenario. The category was unspecified (`meaning` can be cleared as a joke). Leakage via «одалживает». "Dictionary meaning" would flatten the voice. Proposed wording was supplied. | The first linear reading is the test, with an explicit exemption list. Exempt cases map to `correct-as-written` (grammaticality is not correctness), misparses to `misread`, and the problem must name both readings. Abstract scenarios cover paronyms, same-field terms and government. `wrong-phrase` or `grammar`, never `meaning`. Rubric examples come from a published list, mostly the reviewer's. The writer wording is "a meaning it has in standard or colloquial Russian". |
| Reliability / convergence | The verifier lost `misread` and had no ground for exemptions. Corroborated claims skip the verifier. "Restores the speaker" trips the echo check. Misha's line lost protection on an unverified claim, the prompt-only MUST was unenforceable, and the morning has no evidence. The convergence criterion could not fail. The guard fails on today's rubrics. Dev/held-out overlap. No rollback. | `misread` is kept with an explicit mapping. Corroboration is kept (#219) but measured, with a stated fallback. A `misattribution` category with host-enforced grounds keeps `persona-opinion` for genuine reactions; morning posts are exempt. Convergence: approval within two activations without replacement, three morning runs, every new-class blocker judged. Contamination removal is a recorded rubric change. Rollback trigger and procedure are added. |
| Product / brand | A credited live quote and Misha's riff on it could be blocked. Second-reading claims had no verifier exit. The writer rule would flatten slang and irony.
